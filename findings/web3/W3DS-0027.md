## What happened

Between 2026-02-16 and 2026-02-23, five projects lost control of their websites.
The attacker did not break any code. They took the domain names.

For OpenEden and Curvance the domain was transferred from one registrar to
another and the DNS records were pointed at the attacker's hosting. Avantis lost
a Squarespace account after the attacker socially engineered enough billing detail
to pass the account check. Nerochain's domain was redirected. Smithii was a
registrar compromise with the exact route unconfirmed.

In each case a copy of the real site went up, visually identical, with a wallet
drainer built into it. All five delivered the same drainer, AngelFerno.

## Why it worked

Two things, and both are ordinary web problems.

The first is that a registrar has a support desk, and a support desk is a login
made of persuasion. Every technical control a project owns sits behind the domain.
If somebody else can be granted the domain by a phone call, none of those controls
are in the path any more.

The second is the padlock. Fresh certificates were issued the same day, some paid
and some free. A visitor arriving at the fake site saw a valid certificate for the
correct domain name, because it was the correct domain name. There is nothing a
careful user could have noticed. The advice to check the URL, which is what most
security guidance offers, fails completely here.

## The fix, and why the obvious fix is not enough

There is no patch, because nothing was unpatched.

The obvious fix, better security on the registrar account, is necessary and
insufficient, because in the Avantis case the account was reached through the
support process rather than through the password. What actually helps is
narrowing what an attacker gains: a registrar lock so a transfer cannot complete
silently, and a CAA record so a new certificate authority cannot issue for the
domain at all. The padlock is what makes the fake convincing, so denying the
padlock is the control that bites.

And the part worth carrying into the QA lane: the detection here is free. The
certificate transparency logs are public. A project watching its own name in them
learns about the takeover on the day, not when users report their wallets empty.
That method is MTH-W3-010.

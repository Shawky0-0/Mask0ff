## What happened

A wallet app released a new Android version on 2026-06-08. Its signing code built the random
looking number that every signature needs, the nonce, out of the transaction data only.

That number is supposed to be unguessable. It is supposed to come from a secret held in the key.
Here it came from data that anybody can read off the chain.

Between 2026-06-21 and 2026-06-23, 374 wallets were emptied, about 16 million ADA, about
$2.4 million by the source's figure. One published signature per wallet was enough to compute the
private key. No malware, no phishing, no access to the phone.

## Why it worked

A signature is an equation with two secrets in it: the nonce and the key. One equation with two
unknowns cannot be solved. That is the whole trick.

Make the nonce computable and the equation has one unknown. Then it is arithmetic.

The detail worth keeping is where the mistake sat. The secret was passed into the signing function.
It was used. It was just used at the wrong step, after the nonce had already been fixed. Reading
the function signature would tell you the secret was there. Only reading the derivation line tells
you it did not matter.

## The fix, and why the obvious fix would not work

The fix is to derive the nonce from the secret half of the key plus the message, which is what the
standard says and what the reference library does.

The obvious fix, "add more randomness", is wrong for Ed25519 specifically, because the scheme is
deterministic by design and a system that generates the nonce randomly can leak the key a different
way if the randomness is weak or repeats. The correct answer is not more entropy. It is to use the
secret that is already there, at the step where it counts.

The testing lesson is the transferable part. Every functional test passed, because a wrong nonce
still produces a signature that verifies. Only a known answer test against published vectors, or a
line by line read of the derivation, would have caught it. Related:
MTH-W3-002, reading the pipeline rather than the
code, and W3DS-0008, where a wallet's randomness
flag was tested for existence rather than for value.

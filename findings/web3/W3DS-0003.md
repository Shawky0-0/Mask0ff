## What happened

On 2026-07-08 at 20:24 UTC, someone controlling the maintainer account pushed commits straight to
the master branch of the Injective SDK monorepo. Thirty minutes later a version bump triggered the
CI publish job, and between 20:59 and 21:00 UTC all 18 packages in that release train went out as
1.20.21. The payload was a new file, `key-derivation-telemetry.ts`, hooking the two functions every
wallet in that SDK passes through. The moment a wallet was created or loaded, the mnemonic or the
private key was captured and sent out. A revert landed on 2026-07-09 at 05:16 UTC and a clean
1.20.23 followed that evening.

## Why it worked

The attacker broke no code. They needed one account, and the pipeline did the rest. Direct push to
master meant no second pair of eyes. Publish on version bump meant no human between the commit and
the registry. A monorepo release train turned one package into eighteen.

## What the fix is, and why the obvious fix is not enough

The obvious fix is "audit your dependencies". That does not work here, because the version that was
audited is not the version that shipped. Reading the repository before 20:24 UTC or after 05:16 UTC
shows clean code both times.

The fix that works sits in the pipeline: require a pull request, require review on modules that
touch keys, and keep the publish credential separate from the account a developer uses daily. Then
pin by integrity hash on the consumer side, so a republished version cannot quietly replace a known
one. Denying egress from the build environment is the control that kills the class rather than this
instance of it.

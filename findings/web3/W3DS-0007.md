## What happened

The bridge checked cryptographic proofs properly: signatures, state roots, Merkle paths. What it never
checked was whether the value being claimed actually existed anywhere.

The attacker submitted a legitimate 0.01 VRSC transfer, then crafted a fake export transaction
committing to eight transfers payable to their own address. Because they controlled both the transfer
list and the hash that supposedly committed to it, the hash matched. Legitimate notarisations then
relayed that forged export to Ethereum, where every cryptographic check passed and the payouts went
out. About $7.54 million across seven assets.

This was the second time. In May 2026 the same bridge lost $11.6 million. The May attacker negotiated
a return of 4,052.4 ETH for a 1,350 ETH bounty, and on 2026-07-08 the recovered funds were redeposited
into the same bridge contract, where the May patch had only addressed the specific failure mode
identified at the time.

## Why it worked

A hash proves that a document has not changed. It says nothing about whether the document was ever
true. When the same party supplies the list and the commitment to the list, the commitment is
self referential and the verification is theatre.

Everything downstream then behaved honestly. The notaries relayed a validly formed export. The Ethereum
side verified it and paid. Nobody in the chain was compromised and nobody made an error against their
own rules. The rules were simply missing the one check that mattered: does the money exist.

## What the fix is, and why the obvious fix would not work

The obvious fix is the one that was already applied in May: patch the function the attacker used. That
is why there was a July.

The May patch addressed `checkCCEValues` without changing the assumption sitting one layer above it,
that matching hashes imply economic backing. So a second attacker with a different route into the same
assumption found it open. The fix that ends this is a conservation check before payout, and a
serialisation the submitter cannot fully control.

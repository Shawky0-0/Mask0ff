## What happened

Attackers abused a single LayerZero verifier to forge a cross chain message, and minted
116,500 rsETH on Ethereum that nothing backed. Reported loss $230 million. Aave overhauled
its listing standards afterwards.

## Why it matters to a web tester who will never touch a bridge


> System B created something valuable because System A said so, and System B could not
> actually check whether System A said it.

That is a **webhook with no signature check**. It is an **OAuth assertion accepted without
verifying the issuer**. It is **trusting a third party API's response as if it were your own

**The lesson transfers completely. The chain part is decoration.**

## The question this becomes on a normal application

For every inbound message that causes something valuable to happen: **how many independent
parties have to agree before it happens, and can the same attacker compromise all of them?**

If the answer is "one party", the security of the whole flow is the security of that party,
whether it is a verifier, a webhook sender, or an identity provider.

## What happened

Bonzo Finance is a lending market. It priced collateral from a Supra oracle. To accept a price, a
contract checked that the price came with a valid signature from a committee of signers.

The verifier took a committee number from the caller. It used that number to look up the
committee's public key. It never checked that the number pointed at a real committee. When the
number was out of range, the lookup gave back zero.

The attacker then handed in a signature that was also zero. The maths behind BLS signatures treats
zero as the identity element, the value that makes an equation trivially true. Zero signature
against zero key passes. The pairing check answered honestly. It was asked a meaningless question.

With that, the attacker deposited about three dollars of one token, published a price for it twelve
orders of magnitude above the market, and borrowed 6.63 million USDC and 34.5 million WHBAR against
it inside eight seconds.

## Why it worked

Two decisions were missing and either one alone would have stopped it.

First, the lookup was allowed to miss quietly. A read that finds nothing returned zero, and zero
looked like data.

Second, nothing rejected degenerate values before the cryptography ran. A key of zero and a
signature of zero are not a key and a signature. They are the absence of both.

## The fix, and why the obvious fix is not enough

The obvious fix is to bounds check the committee identifier. That closes this path. It does not
close the class, because there are other ways a key can end up empty: a configuration that failed
to load, a migration that left a row blank, a rotation that deleted the old key before writing the
new one.

The control that kills the class is the second one they added: refuse the identity element, and
refuse an off curve point, before you evaluate anything. Say no to the empty value at the door.

## What happened

Ostium used a pull oracle: prices are written on chain only when a trade needs one. The
`PrivatePriceUpKeep` contract took a price report with a signature, verified the signature against
an authorised signer set, and wrote the price. According to the writeup, the documentation described
the Gelato automation network as the only address authorised to trigger these actions.

On 2026-07-15 an attacker reported Bitcoin at $5,000 and then at $60,000 inside a single atomic
transaction, opened leveraged positions at the low price, closed them at the high price, and
extracted about $23.75 million from the liquidity vault. A twelve times move that never happened in
any real market, accepted twice in one transaction.

## Why it worked

The signature check did its job. It proved the report came from an authorised signer. It cannot
prove the number in the report is true, and nothing else tried to. There was no bound against the
previous price, no staleness rule, and no second source to disagree.

This is worth being precise about, because it is the part that transfers. Authorisation and
plausibility are two different properties. A system that only checks the first one has no defence at
all once the authorised party is compromised, coerced, or simply wrong. There is no partial failure
mode: it accepts whatever it is told.

## What the fix is, and why the obvious fix would not work

The obvious fix is to secure the forwarder better, or narrow who may call it. That is what happened
after the November 2025 audit: the specific example Zellic illustrated got patched, and the auditors
had already said in writing that their examples were not the full list.

Narrowing the caller does not help, because the caller was never the problem. The problem is a
value accepted with no second opinion. The fix is a bound: cap the deviation per update, reject
stale reports, read a second independent source, and halt when the two disagree rather than picking
one. That holds even if the forwarder is fully compromised, which is the only assumption worth
designing against.
